How it fits together

Nothing on the estate works alone

The businesses are separate, the land is not. Each one feeds the next, which is why we manage them as one estate.

  1. Coppice the woods

    Coppicing lets light reach the woodland floor, bringing back violets, primroses and the rare butterflies that feed on them.

  2. Season the timber

    Logs dry for up to a year in our sheds. The brash and branches fuel the estate's 200kW biomass boiler.

  3. Warm the house

    That boiler heats Halecat's homes, studios and workshops, from wood regrown on the same estate.

  4. Press the harvest

    Apples and damsons come in from 21 orchards grazed by rare breed sheep, and are pressed by hand up at Halecat.

The land

Between the Winster and the Scar

Witherslack Hall Estate sits on the edge of the Winster Valley in Southern Lakeland, bordered to the west by the River Winster and to the east by Whitbarrow Scar.

It holds the largest area of semi natural woodland in Cumbria. Much of it is designated as Sites of Special Scientific Interest and managed to the standards of the Forestry Commission and Butterfly Conservation. Since 2016 we have worked with the Westmorland Red Squirrel Society to bring red squirrels back to Witherslack.
